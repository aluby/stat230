Overview

The final project is an opportunity to use the methods learned in this course to address a question of your choice through data analysis.

You will work in groups of 2 or 3 people assigned by me with your input. The expectation is that each student shares equal responsibility for the report. In a group project, you will turn in one report. Please let me know if you are having trouble communicating with your partner.

Due dates:

  • Monday of Week 8: Project partner form
  • Friday of Week 9: Study proposal due
  • Wednesday of Week 10: Data presentations
  • Monday end of term: Final report due
    • 1% extra credit for submitting to USCLAP (requires a rough draft due the last day of class and using a dataset not from our list)
    • 1% extra credit for submitting final report 24 hours early
    • Extra credit on last HW if you meet with Noah at least once as a group to discuss either your study proposal or final report

Stage 1: Study Proposal

Identify the research questions and data that will guide your project. Your dataset should have at least 100 observations/cases and at least 5 variables (at least four predictor variables and a response variable). You should not use textbook data. You should carefully consider the questions that you intend to ask with your data, as well as the design of the study and how the data were or will be collected. It is essential that the methods used to analyze your dataset ultimately be appropriate to both the question of interest and the data at hand.

Examples of previous project abstracts will be posted on Moodle to give you an idea of what kinds of things people have done in the past. I will also post some online sources of data (but you may obtain data from a source not listed there).

You should submit:

  1. A paragraph briefly identifying the research questions and data you will use. Be explicit about which variables will be included in your analysis, which variable is the response and which are the predictors. Describe how your data were obtained, providing a link or citation if appropriate.
  • You should not use textbook data for this project. One goal of this project is for you to seek out data from an outside source to answer a question that you are interested in.
  • I discourage you from using data found on Kaggle, since many datasets uploaded to that website do not have appropriate documentation for how they were collected (and often are fake datasets). If you choose to analyze a dataset found on Kaggle, you should be sure that you have appropriate information about where the data came from.
  • Please keep in mind that many datasets have complex structures that require methods that are more advanced that we have learned in this class. A few kinds of data that generally won’t work well for this project because there will usually be a violation of the independence assumption of our models: – Time series data (measurements of a single observational unit over time) – Spatial data – Longitudinal data (data with repeated measurements on the same individual). (Note: this can work if you consider averaging the repeated measurements or only looking at the measurements at one or two time points)

  1. One informative and well-labeled plot or table along with a paragraph that gives a brief description. This is not supposed to be an exhaustive analysis. The purpose is for you to ensure that your data are in a format that is ready for analysis and begin to think about modeling issues that may arise.

  2. A link to your data. The dataset should be in a format readable by R.

We will do “data presentations” on the last day of class.

Stage 2: Final Report

We will follow the USCLAP guidelines for the format of the report: https://www.causeweb.org/usproc/usclap

Data Presentation/Lightning Talk

Each group will have 3 minutes to present and 2 minutes for questions/transitions. This is extremely short! You will not have time to discuss your progress in any amount of detail, so you should focus on your data and primary modeling approach.

Slide 1: Data overview

  • Source?
  • Response/predictor variables?
  • General research question?

Slide 2: Modeling approach

  • Theoretical equation with your response and predictors
  • Can include transformations, but don’t need to show in detail
  • Do not talk about the validity of the model

Slide 3: 1 result/challenge so far:

This can be anything you’ve spent some time thinking about! Some ideas:

  • Cool EDA showing the relationship you will estimate
  • Data issue you ran into
  • Fun paper that you read as background information
  • Report 1-2 interesting coefficients related to your research question
  • Discuss an influential point and whether or not you will remove it
  • Discuss a problematic residual plot and what you will do to fix it

Format

  • 3 pages or less (including figures but not including references). You may include up to 2 pages of appendices of graphs or coefficient tables that do not count towards this limit. Note that these should be supporting graphs, not discussed in the main text.
  • Introduction: this is where you introduce the problem and why it is important to study. This should focus on the data and the “real-world” questions. Give enough background in the intro so that a reader doesn’t need to refer to source material, but take care not to plagiarize your source material above. Cite sources as needed.
  • Data and EDA: this is where you discuss the variables of interest and present your EDA (e.g. graphs) to motivate your model. This section should not be exhaustive but should include key figures that help the reader understand your data and “see what’s coming” in the results section.
  • Results: this is where you present your model selection approach, final model, and interpret the results. You should explain your results both statistically and in context.
  • Discussion: this is a shorter section that summarizes the main points of your analysis and explains your answer to the research question.
  • Appendices: this is an optional section where you can include graphs or results that you used, but aren’t central to the point you are trying to make in the remainder of the report. (Eg residual plots that were “normal” or a list of transformations that you considered)

Best practices for small group work

You will work in groups of two or three for this project.

While you are permitted to do some work separately (e.g., the homework components, revising a section at a time when your schedules don’t permit meeting together), I discourage you from completing substantial portions of the final report individually. Each partner should contribute substantively to each component of the report, and each partner is assumed to be an equal contributor to the final product. You should be in regular contact with your partner(s) and should meet at least once outside of class. If you are having issues communicating with your partner(s), please email me as soon as possible.

Academic Integrity

For this case study, you may use the following resources:

  • Your own course notes (from any class!) and any Stat230 materials of your own or provided by Amanda
  • Web searches/AI where your search/prompts are general questions about coding (e.g., how do I subset a data set in R where I want only certain numerical values?). If you use AI, you MUST provide a document shows your “conversation.” Failure to do so will be considered academic dishonesty.
  • Web searches/AI where your search/prompts are background research about your project topic. If you use AI, you MUST provide a document shows your “conversation.” You must also find a real source (published by a reputable source, ideally peer-reviewed) and don’t rely purely on AI. Failure to do so will be considered academic dishonesty.
  • Web searches/AI where your search/prompts are general questions about formatting (e.g., how do I make a table in GoogleDocs?). If you use AI, you MUST provide a document shows your “conversation.” Failure to do so will be considered academic dishonesty
  • The writing center for help editing your write up.
  • Your in-class group
  • Amanda
  • Noah
  • StatLab / Math Skills Center for R coding help or help with statistical models

For this project you may NOT use the following resources:

  • Other humans (including other folks in this class)
  • Web searches/AI where your searches/prompts are the specific questions you are being asked in the project about coding (e.g., How do I fit a linear regression model in R with the sales price as the response variable and square footage as the predictor variable?).
  • Web searches/AI where your search/prompts are the specific formatting questions you are being asked to do for the project (e.g., please write a caption for this table).
  • AI to write or edit any portion of your original write-up or your final write-up.

Rubric

Note: I recommend going through this checklist as a team before your final submission

  • Title and authors (5%)
  • Abstract (5%)
  • Introduction (10%)
  • Data and EDA (15%)
  • Results (40%)
      • A complete model statement is given (give the equation!)
      • An appropriate model is chosen
      • Model assumptions are assessed and satisfactory
      • Estimates and uncertainties (confidence intervals) of model coefficients are given and both are interpreted in context
      • The model and estimates are interpreted correctly and in the context of the question being addressed
      • Technical language is used correctly
  • Discussion (15%)
  • Quarto appendix (separate file) (5%)
  • Overall presentation (10%)
