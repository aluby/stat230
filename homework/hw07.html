Probabilities can sometimes be difficult to interpret. If a probability changes from .01 to .02, the difference is .01 but the probability has also doubled. If a probability changes from .5 to .51, the difference is still .01 but this increase in probability is only a 2% increase. Because of this, we often report the odds:

\[\text{Odds}(Y=1) = \frac{P(Y=1)}{1-P(Y=1)} = \frac{P(Y=1)}{P(Y=0)}\]

  1. Calculate the odds of being employed for those in the training group

  2. Calculate the odds of being employed for those in the control group

  3. The odds ratio tells us how much the odds of success change when we move from one group (eg the control) to the other (eg the training group). Calculate the odds ratio for comparing the training group to the control group.

  4. Complete the following sentence:

“The odds of being employed for those who completed the training program are ________ times the odds of being employed for those in the control group.”

  1. If the Training Program had no effect at all on employment, what would you expect the Odds Ratio to be? Why?
