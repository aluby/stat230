Problem 1: Log transformed response

In class, we talked about how we must be careful about interpreting estimated \(\beta\) coefficient when we’ve transformed the variables since the relationship is typically no longer constant for the entire range of the predictor variable. (e.g. the wildfires example, which decreases from 1960-1980-ish and then increases at a non-constant rate) One exception is log transformations, which we can usually “math-out” a reasonable interpretation on the original variable scale.

A data science team at a tech startup is analyzing the relationship between their weekly digital advertising budget and their weekly app downloads. Because both advertising budgets and user growth often scale multiplicatively rather than additively, the team decides to test models using the natural logarithm.

  • Let \(Y\) = Downloads (in thousands of users)
  • Let \(X\) = Ad_Spend (in thousands of dollars)

  1. Show that, if we use the fitted regression equation \(\widehat{ \log y_i} = \widehat{\beta_0} + \widehat{\beta_1} x\), then \(\hat{y_i} = e^{\widehat{\beta_0}} e^{\widehat{\beta_1} x_i}\)

  2. Use part (a) to show or explain why this means that we can interpret \(\widehat{\beta_1}\) as follows: “When \(x\) increases by 1 unit, we expect \(y\) to multiply by \(\widehat{\beta_1}\)”

  3. If we find the following fitted equation \(\widehat{\log y} = 2.4 + 0.08 x\), provide an interpretation of \(\widehat{\beta_1}\) in terms of percentage change in raw downloads
