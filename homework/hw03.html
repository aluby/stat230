Problem 1: Fri

The child_speaks1 data are from a study about whether there is a relationship between the age at which a child first speaks (in months) and his/her score on the Gesell aptitude test taken later in childhood. Higher scores on this test indicate age appropriate levels of motor skills, language, social, and cognitive abilities; while lower scores identify children who may need extra support.

child_speaks <- read_csv("https://aluby.github.io/stat230/data/ChildSpeaks.csv")

  2. Produce a scatterplot of these data, and comment on whether age of first speaking appears to be a good explainer of Gesell aptitude score

  3. Fit and report the linear regression equation and the value of \(R^2\) (with an interpretation).

  4. Which child has the largest (in absolute value) residual? Explain what is unusual about that child

  5. Remove the child you identified in (d) and repeat parts (b) and (c). Comment on how removing the one child has affected the line and the value of \(R^2\)

  6. Now, go back to the original dataset. Instead of removing the large residual child, remove the child who took 42 months to speak. Repeat parts (b) and (c) and comment on how removing the one child has affected the line and the value of \(R^2\).

Statistics students completed a class project where they designed an experiment to explore the relationship between the wing length (in cm) and flight time (in seconds) of paper helicopters. One group collected data from six different wing lengths.

flights <- read.csv("https://aluby.github.io/stat230/data/winglengths2.csv")

  1. Create a scatterplot of flight time against wing length. Describe what you learn from this plot.

  2. Fit a simple linear regression model of flight time against wing length. Create residual plots to explore the adequacy of your model. Report your results.

  3. Try several transformations of either the response or the explanatory variable to see if you can alleviate the problems you observed in part (b). Report the “best” transformation and whether it remedies the issues.

  4. It is likely that you cannot successfully find a transformation to solve the nonlinearity problem. A closer look at the residuals helps to explain why. The residuals from the regression follow a somewhat sinusoidal pattern: down, then up, then down again. This is a pattern that is seen in a typical third-degree polynomial.
