Motivating data: number of anti-discrimination laws

The equality_index data includes the observed number of anti-discrimination laws in each state, \(Y\) . We are interested in modeling \(Y\) by a state’s percent_urban and its historical presidential voting patterns. The historical variable has 3 levels:

  • dem = the state typically votes for the Democratic candidate
  • gop = the state typically votes for the Republican (GOP) candidate
  • swing = the state often swings back and forth between Democratic and Republican candidates

Source: the {bayesrules} R package

EDA

  • laws is skewed
  • as percent urban increases, laws also increases
  • dem and swing generally have more laws than gop

EDA (log scale)

  • log(laws) is still a little skewed
  • log(laws) and percent_urban is more linear
  • if we had states with 0 laws, might have to do log(laws + 1)

Link function

How can we relate the expectation of \(Y\) and our linear predictor?

\(\log(\lambda) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_1 + \cdots + \beta_p X_p = \eta\)

Poisson regression model for counts

Random component: \(Y_i = \#\) anti-discrimination laws in a state; Poisson distribution is reasonable

. . .


Link function: \(E(Y) = \lambda\) and \(\lambda >0\)

\(\Longrightarrow \log(\lambda) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_1 + \cdots + \beta_p X_p = \eta\)

. . .


Inverse-link function: \(E(Y) = e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X_1 + \cdots + \beta_p X_p}\)

How can we interpret the coefficients?

Interpreting the coefficients

\(\log(\widehat{\lambda}) = \widehat{\beta}_0 + \widehat{\beta}_1 x\)

How does the expected (average) number of events, \(\widehat{\lambda}\), change for a 1-unit increase in \(x\)?

. . .

Let’s consider \(x=0\) and \(x=1\):

  • \(\widehat{\lambda} = e^{\widehat{\beta}_0 + \widehat{\beta}_1(0)}=e^{\widehat{\beta}_0}\)

  • \(\widehat{\lambda} = e^{\widehat{\beta}_0 + \widehat{\beta}_1(1)}=e^{\widehat{\beta}_0} e^{\widehat{\beta}_1}\)

. . .

The expectation at \(x+1\) is \(e^{\widehat{\beta}_1}\) times the expectation at \(x\)

Interpreting the coefficients

In our example: \(\log(\widehat{\lambda}) = -2.18 + 0.06 \cdot \tt{percent urban}\)

. . .

\(e^{0.06} \approx 1.06\)

. . .


→ A 1 percent increase in the percent of urban residents is associated with an increase in the average number of antidiscrimination laws by a factor of 1.06 (i.e., a 6% increase).
