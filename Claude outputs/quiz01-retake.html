Questions

Model statements (10 pts)

  1. Write out the theoretical simple linear regression model relating repair_cost to age, using correct notation and defining any Greek letters you use.
  1. Using the output above, write out the fitted regression equation, in context (i.e., using age and repair_cost, not \(x\) and \(y\)).

Interpreting the output (15 pts)

  1. Interpret the estimated slope in the context of this problem. Your answer should be a complete sentence.
  1. The residual standard error is reported in the output above. State its value (with units) and interpret what it tells us about the typical prediction from this model.

Inference for the slope (20 pts)

  1. State the null and alternative hypotheses for testing whether age is a significant linear predictor of repair cost.
  1. Using the summary() output above, report the test statistic and p-value for this test, and state your conclusion in context (use \(\alpha = 0.05\)).
  1. Using the summary() output above and the table of \(t^*\) values at the end of this quiz, construct a 90% confidence interval for the true slope relating age to repair cost. Interpret this interval in one sentence, in context.
  1. Without redoing any calculations, explain whether your interval from part (c) is consistent with your conclusion in part (b).

Checking the LINE conditions (25 pts)

For each condition below, circle whether it is reasonably met for this model, based on the plots above.

For whichever condition you marked No, describe the specific visual evidence in the plot that led you to that conclusion. (If you marked all four “Yes,” pick the condition you’re least confident about and justify it instead.)

Confidence and prediction intervals (30 pts)

  1. One of the two shaded bands above is a confidence band and the other is a prediction band. Which is which, and how can you tell them apart on the graph?
  1. Using the graph, estimate the 95% interval for the average monthly repair cost among all 6-year-old machines. Is this a confidence interval or a prediction interval?
  1. A shop manager asks for a 95% interval for the repair cost of one specific machine that is 6 years old. Would this interval be wider or narrower than your answer to part (b)? Explain why, without doing any calculation.
  1. The chain also owns one vintage machine that is 20 years old. Would you trust this model to predict its monthly repair cost? Why or why not?
